## In short

A drone can now take camera images in Gazebo with the true answer saved next to each one, and a first detector finds the hostile drone against the sky and estimates its distance and direction. On the three images where it found the drone, the distance was within about 20 % and the direction within about 0.6°. It was built on `feat-yolo` and is now merged into `dev`.

## What was done

| # | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Save camera images from Gazebo as PNGs, from the host (`snap`) or from the shell (`snap(id, count)`) | `swarm_backend/datagen/save_images.py`, `mount-backend.sh`, `shell.py` |
| 2 | Save the truth with every image: positions of the camera drone and the other drones, the camera's orientation, and the distance, direction and pixel of every other drone | `swarm_backend/sim/ground_truth.py` |
| 3 | Scenario 1: two gimbal drones, drone 1 flies circles, drone 2 chases it, RViz shows both cameras | `scenarios/scenario1.sh`, `scenarios/scenario1.yaml` |

| 6 | `predict`: run a detector on saved images, compare with the truth, draw the result, write a CSV and plots. Method `baseline` works, `yolo` is a placeholder | `swarm_backend/detect/predict.py` |
| 7 | Only one swarm shell can run at a time (two would fight over the drones) | `shell.py` |
| 8 | OpenCV added to the container image | `docker/Dockerfile` |

Unit tests without ROS: `tests/test_candidates.py`, `tests/test_camera.py`, `tests/test_ground_truth.py`.

Against the plan's order of work: step 1 (camera math) is partly done, step 3 (baseline detector) is done for single images. Step 2 (a live detection node) and tracking over several images are not started.

## Merged with Olle's work

Olle had pushed his own detection work to the same branch on 9 Oct: a baseline detector, k-in-a-row tracking (`detect/tracking.py`), a live detector on a drone's camera (`detect/live.py`, `./mount-backend.sh detect`) and a detection flight test (`detecttest`). After the merge:

- **One detector.** His live detector and flight test now run our colour-based detector (`detect/candidates.py`). His first detector and his offline `annotate` tool are removed (`predict` does that job). On the 10 scenario-1 images, with clouds and a rolled camera, it gave 20 boxes per image; ours gave one correct box in three images and none in the rest.
- **His tracking and live parts are kept as they were.**
- `detect/camera.py` is one shared camera model (his intrinsics and horizon row, plus pixel → direction).
- The live detector no longer needs `--horizon`; it is still there as an option. The options `--k-sigma` and `--min-contrast` are replaced by `--contrast`.
- The live detector ran against the simulation after the change (about 220 ms per image on the Mac with Gazebo running). The flight test `detecttest` has not been flown with the new detector.

## Results so far

**First 15 images** (manual flight, drone mostly against the sky): the baseline found the drone in 13, with no false alarms. It found it at every size from 2 to 13 px. The two misses were a drone of 1–2 px. The settings were tuned on these same images, so this is not a fair score.

**10 images from scenario 1**, with truth:

| Image | Size | Estimated distance | True distance | Direction error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| …172.296 | 29 px | 7.1 m | 8.9 m | 0.6° |
| …175.300 | 19 px | 10.8 m | 9.8 m | 0.5° |
| …178.300 | 18 px | 11.4 m | 10.9 m | 0.5° |

The other 7 were missed, as expected for this detector: in 6 the drone is in front of trees or ground, and in 1 it is 8 m away, about 45 px wide (over the 40 px size limit) and across the horizon.

## What we learned

- **The baseline only works against the sky.** In scenario 1 the chaser flies at the target's height and closes to 5–10 m, so the target is mostly in front of the ground. For baseline tests, fly the target higher than the chaser, or keep more distance.
- **Blue gaps between white clouds look like dark spots.** The first version gave up to 31 false alarms per image. Requiring the spot to be dark in absolute terms (not only darker than its surroundings) removed them.
- **The drone sees itself.** Its own arms and propellers are in the top of the picture. Anything cut off by the image edge is ignored.
- **The picture is tilted.** The camera rolls with the drone (about 30° in a hard chase); the gimbal does not keep the horizon level in the simulation.
- **The truth is good to about 0.7°** (about 5 px). The positions are taken within about 5 ms of the image and the chaser turns fast. A direction error under 1° is as good as the truth can show.
- **The direction still cheated on one thing:** it used the camera's true orientation from the truth file. Since 14 Oct the live detector reads it from PX4's gimbal status (0.35° median error).
- **The chase needs a close radar to be watchable.** With the mock radar 2 km away, the chaser stays about 59 m back and the target is 3–4 px. Scenario 1 puts the radar 100 m away, so the chaser closes to 10 m.
- **The simulation is slow on the Mac** with two cameras and two RViz windows (about 7 % of real time). Scenario 1 flies at 20 m instead of 50 m and allows the climb 20 minutes. `--no-view` skips RViz.
